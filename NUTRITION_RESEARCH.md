## 1. Ndolé

### 1. Basic Information
* **English name:** Ndole / Bitterleaf Soup
* **Local names:** Ndolé
* **Alternative spellings:** N'dole, Ndoleh
* **Scientific name (Bitterleaf):** *Vernonia amygdalina*
* **Food category:** Soups / Traditional Dishes
* **Typical meal type:** Lunch, Dinner, Festival
* **Country:** Cameroon
* **Regions:** Littoral (origin: Sawa people), but eaten nationwide.

### 2. Food Description
* **Ingredients:** Washed bitterleaf (*Vernonia amygdalina*), boiled peanuts (groundnuts), crayfish, palm oil or refined oil, garlic, ginger, onions, meat, and/or dried fish/prawns.
* **Traditional preparation:** Peanuts are boiled and blended into a paste. Bitterleaf is washed multiple times to remove excess bitterness. The paste is cooked with aromatics, meat/fish, and finished with oil.
* **Modern/Variations:** Often made with refined vegetable oil instead of palm oil to reduce saturated fat. Sometimes spinach is mixed with bitterleaf if bitterleaf is scarce.

### 3. Standard Serving Sizes
* **Small:** 150g (about 1/2 cup) - typically when served as a side.
* **Medium:** 300g (about 1 cup) - standard meal portion.
* **Large:** 450g (1.5 cups) - festival or heavy dinner portion.

### 4. Nutrition Facts (Medium Serving - 300g)
*Estimates based on traditional preparation with beef and palm oil.*
* **Calories:** 420 kcal
* **Protein:** 28g
* **Carbohydrates:** 15g
* **Fat:** 30g
* **Fiber:** 8g
* **Sugar:** 2g
* **Sodium:** 450mg (highly variable based on bouillon/Maggi used)
* **Micronutrients:** High in Iron, Calcium, Potassium, Vitamin A (if palm oil is used), Zinc.

### 5. Micronutrient Highlights
* **Vitamin A:** From unrefined red palm oil, crucial for vision and immune function.
* **Iron:** From the bitterleaf and meat/fish, essential for preventing anemia.
* **Healthy Fats:** Peanuts provide monounsaturated fats.

### 6. Health Benefits
* **Satiety:** Very high due to the combination of protein (meat/fish), fat (peanuts/oil), and fiber (bitterleaf).
* **Digestion:** Bitterleaf contains compounds that traditional medicine suggests aid digestion and gut health.
* **Heart Health:** Peanuts provide heart-healthy unsaturated fats, though this is offset if excessive palm oil is used.

### 7. Potential Concerns
* **High Calorie Density:** The combination of peanuts and added oil makes it very calorie-dense.
* **Sodium:** High reliance on bouillon cubes (Maggi) can drastically increase sodium levels, raising blood pressure risks.
* **Saturated Fat:** If excessive palm oil is used.

### 8. Goal Compatibility
* **Weight Loss:** Moderate. It is filling and low-carb, but calorie-dense. Portion control is essential.
* **Weight Gain:** Excellent. Nutrient-dense and calorie-dense.
* **Muscle Building:** Excellent. High protein from meat, fish, and peanuts.
* **Healthy Eating:** Good. Rich in vegetables and nuts, but oil and sodium must be monitored.
* **Diabetes-Friendly:** Excellent. Low glycemic index; does not spike blood sugar.

### 9. Satiety Analysis
* **Satiety Score (1-10):** 9
* **Explanation:** The trifecta of plant fiber (bitterleaf), protein (meat/fish), and fat (peanuts) severely slows gastric emptying, keeping the user full for hours.

### 10. Health Score
* **Health Score (1-10):** 8
* **Explanation:** A nutritional powerhouse of vitamins, minerals, and macronutrients, only losing points due to typical over-use of oil and sodium.

### 11. Best Pairings
* **Traditional:** Boiled plantains, Bobolo, or Miondo.
* **Healthier:** Boiled unripe plantains or boiled yams (adds resistant starch, lowers GI).

### 12. Better Alternatives
* **Preparation Alternative:** Use less oil, rely on the natural fats from the peanuts, and reduce bouillon cubes.

### 13. Recommended Frequency
* **Frequency:** 2-3 times/week.
* **Explanation:** Perfectly healthy for regular consumption if portion and oil are controlled.

### 14. Preparation Intelligence
* **Boiled/Stewed:** Traditional method retains most nutrients. Over-washing the bitterleaf can strip water-soluble vitamins, but is necessary for palatability.

### 15. Meal Intelligence
* **Commonly eaten for:** Lunch, Dinner, Festivals.
* **Explanation:** It is a heavy, slow-digesting meal usually eaten as the main meal of the day.

### 16. Portion Intelligence
* **Mistakes:** Eating a large bowl of Ndolé with multiple sticks of Bobolo. The calories from the oil + peanuts + dense carbs (Bobolo) can easily exceed 1000 kcal in a single sitting.

### 17. AI Coaching Insights
* "Ndolé is highly nutritious but calorie-dense; scoop less oil when serving to save calories."
* "Pairing Ndolé with boiled unripe plantain keeps your blood sugar stable for longer."
* "The peanuts and meat in Ndolé provide excellent protein for muscle recovery."

### 18. Confidence Assessment
* **Confidence:** Medium-High
* **Explanation:** Ingredients are standard, but actual fat and sodium content vary drastically by cook.

## 2. Puff Puff (Beignets)

### 1. Basic Information
* **English name:** Fried Dough / Beignets
* **Local names:** Puff Puff, Makate
* **Food category:** Street Foods / Snacks
* **Typical meal type:** Breakfast, Snack
* **Country:** Cameroon (and across West Africa)

### 2. Food Description
* **Ingredients:** Flour, yeast, sugar, water, pinch of salt. Deep-fried in vegetable oil.
* **Traditional preparation:** Dough is mixed, left to rise, and then dropped by hand into hot deep-frying oil until golden brown.

### 3. Standard Serving Sizes
* **Small:** 3 pieces (approx. 90g)
* **Medium:** 5 pieces (approx. 150g)
* **Large:** 8+ pieces (approx. 240g+)

### 4. Nutrition Facts (Medium Serving - 150g / 5 pieces)
* **Calories:** 450 kcal
* **Protein:** 8g
* **Carbohydrates:** 65g
* **Fat:** 18g
* **Fiber:** 2g
* **Sugar:** 15g
* **Sodium:** 150mg

### 5. Micronutrient Highlights
* Very few micronutrients. Mostly empty calories. Some B-vitamins if fortified flour is used.

### 6. Health Benefits
* **Energy:** Provides rapid, highly accessible energy (simple carbs).

### 7. Potential Concerns
* **High Calorie Density:** Extremely dense in calories for its volume.
* **Deep Fried Oils:** Often fried in reused vegetable oils, containing trans fats or oxidized fats.
* **High Glycemic Index:** Spikes blood sugar rapidly, leading to a crash and subsequent hunger.
* **Low Satiety:** Easy to overeat without feeling full.

### 8. Goal Compatibility
* **Weight Loss:** Poor. High in calories, low in satiety.
* **Weight Gain:** Good (for pure calories), but Poor (for health/body composition).
* **Muscle Building:** Poor. Lacks adequate protein.
* **Diabetes-Friendly:** Poor. Spikes blood sugar rapidly.

### 9. Satiety Analysis
* **Satiety Score (1-10):** 3
* **Explanation:** Rapid digestion of simple refined flour and sugar means hunger returns quickly.

### 10. Health Score
* **Health Score (1-10):** 2
* **Explanation:** Refined carbs, sugar, and deep-frying oil offer very little nutritional value.

### 11. Best Pairings
* **Traditional:** Beans (Haricot) or Pap (Akamu).
* **Healthier:** Pairing with Beans is highly recommended as the fiber and protein from the beans slow down the digestion of the Puff Puff.

### 12. Better Alternatives
* **Alternative:** Roasted plantain, boiled groundnuts, or fresh fruit for snacks.

### 13. Recommended Frequency
* **Frequency:** Rarely / Occasional
* **Explanation:** Best kept as an occasional treat rather than a daily breakfast staple.

### 14. Preparation Intelligence
* **Fried:** Oil absorption depends heavily on oil temperature. Frying at too low a temperature makes them absorb significantly more fat.

### 15. Meal Intelligence
* **Commonly eaten for:** Breakfast, Street Food Snack.

### 16. Portion Intelligence
* **Mistakes:** Buying a "1000 FCFA" bag and eating it mindlessly. Often paired with sugary drinks (e.g., Folere with heavy sugar), creating a massive sugar load.

### 17. AI Coaching Insights
* "Puff Puff gives quick energy but leaves you hungry later. Pair it with beans to stay full!"
* "Beignets are deep-fried; enjoying them occasionally rather than daily supports heart health."

### 18. Confidence Assessment
* **Confidence:** High
* **Explanation:** The recipe is highly standardized and macronutrient profiles of fried dough are well-documented globally.

## 3. Eru

### 1. Basic Information
* **English name:** Eru / Okazi
* **Local names:** Eru
* **Scientific name:** *Gnetum africanum* (Eru leaves), *Talinum triangulare* (Waterleaf)
* **Food category:** Soups / Traditional Dishes
* **Typical meal type:** Lunch, Dinner
* **Country:** Cameroon (Origin: Manyu Division, South West)

### 2. Food Description
* **Ingredients:** Finely shredded Eru leaves, waterleaf (or spinach), red palm oil, crayfish, smoked fish, cow skin (kanda), beef.
* **Traditional preparation:** Waterleaf is cooked down to release its water, then the tough Eru leaves are added. Generous amounts of palm oil, meat, and crayfish are stirred in. Water is rarely added.

### 3. Standard Serving Sizes
* **Medium:** 250g (approx. 1 cup)

### 4. Nutrition Facts (Medium Serving - 250g)
* **Calories:** 450 kcal
* **Protein:** 25g
* **Carbohydrates:** 10g
* **Fat:** 35g
* **Fiber:** 12g
* **Sugar:** 1g
* **Sodium:** 350mg

### 5. Micronutrient Highlights
* **Dietary Fiber:** Exceptionally high due to the tough Eru leaves.
* **Vitamin A & E:** From unrefined red palm oil.
* **Iron & Calcium:** High concentrations from the dark leafy greens.

### 6. Health Benefits
* **Satiety & Gut Health:** The massive fiber content supports digestion (though the toughness of Eru requires a healthy gut to process).
* **Nutrient Density:** Provides a vast array of fat-soluble vitamins.

### 7. Potential Concerns
* **Extreme Fat Content:** Eru traditionally relies heavily on palm oil for its texture and taste, making it incredibly calorie-dense.
* **Saturated Fat:** Palm oil is high in saturated fat.

### 8. Goal Compatibility
* **Weight Loss:** Moderate. It is extremely filling (great for weight loss), but the oil must be strictly controlled.
* **Muscle Building:** Excellent. Dense in protein from assorted meats/fish.
* **Diabetes-Friendly:** Excellent. Very low carbs, high fiber.

### 9. Satiety Analysis
* **Satiety Score (1-10):** 10
* **Explanation:** The combination of highly fibrous leaves, fat, and protein makes Eru one of the most satiating meals available.

### 10. Health Score
* **Health Score (1-10):** 7
* **Explanation:** Nutritionally incredible, but heavily penalized for the massive amounts of palm oil typically used.

### 11. Best Pairings
* **Traditional:** Water Fufu (fermented cassava) or Garri.
* **Healthier:** Eating with a smaller ball of Water Fufu to control total calories.

### 12. Better Alternatives
* **Preparation Alternative:** Use half the normal amount of palm oil. The dish will be less glossy but significantly lower in calories.

### 13. Recommended Frequency
* **Frequency:** 1-2 times/week.
* **Explanation:** Due to its heavy nature and high fat content, it's a superb meal but shouldn't be eaten every single day.

### 14. Preparation Intelligence
* The leaves are tough and require proper cooking to break down enough for comfortable digestion.

### 15. Meal Intelligence
* **Commonly eaten for:** Lunch, Dinner, Celebrations.

### 16. Portion Intelligence
* **Mistakes:** The oil pooling at the bottom of the plate contains hundreds of calories. Avoid scooping up the excess oil.

### 17. AI Coaching Insights
* "Eru is packed with fiber and keeps you full for hours! Just watch the palm oil."
* "Try leaving the excess oil on the plate instead of eating it to save a massive amount of calories."

### 18. Confidence Assessment
* **Confidence:** Medium
* **Explanation:** The oil quantity poured by individual cooks creates massive variance in the final calorie count.
